# Valorant Economy — Credits, Buy Rounds and Money Discipline

Prices and rewards below reflect training data (late 2025 / early 2026). Riot
adjusts weapon prices occasionally, so treat exact numbers as approximate.

## Credit basics

- Everyone starts round 1 (and round 13 after side swap) with 800 credits — the
  pistol round.
- Credit cap is 9,000. Banking beyond the cap wastes income.
- Round win: 3,000 credits per player.
- Round loss: 1,900 for the first loss, 2,400 for the second consecutive loss,
  2,900 for the third and beyond (the "loss streak bonus").
- Surviving a lost round without dying pays only 1,000 — saving your gun costs
  your team loss-bonus money, so only save when the weapon is worth it.
- Each kill pays 200 credits to the killer.
- Planting the spike pays +300 to every attacker, even if the round is lost.
  Always plant when it is safe — it is free money and forces defender utility.

## Weapon prices (approximate)

- Sidearms: Classic free, Shorty 300, Frenzy 450, Ghost 500, Sheriff 800.
- SMGs: Stinger 950, Spectre 1,600.
- Shotguns: Bucky 850, Judge 1,850.
- Rifles: Bulldog 2,050, Guardian 2,250, Phantom 2,900, Vandal 2,900.
- Snipers: Marshal 950, Outlaw 2,400, Operator 4,700.
- Machine guns: Ares 1,600, Odin 3,200.
- Shields: Light 400 (25 shield), Regen 650 (recharging pool), Heavy 1,000
  (50 shield).

## The standard buy cycle

- **Pistol round**: common choices are Classic + light shield + utility, Ghost +
  no shield, or Frenzy + light. Utility often beats upgraded pistols for the
  team as a whole.
- **Bonus round (round 2 after winning pistol)**: winners often play Spectres +
  shields against the losers' forced or saved buy.
- **Round 2 after losing pistol**: either full save (eco) into a round-3 full buy,
  or a coordinated force buy (Sheriffs/Stingers + light shields) to punish the
  bonus. Half-and-half team buys are the worst option — commit together.
- **Full buy**: rifle + heavy shield + full utility, roughly 3,900–4,700 credits
  depending on agent. Don't full buy unless most of the team can.

## Buy-round vocabulary

- **Eco / save**: spend almost nothing (Classic, maybe light armor) to guarantee
  a future full buy. Play for exit kills, spike plant money, and weapon steals.
- **Force buy**: spend everything you have when a save wouldn't reach a full buy
  anyway, or when the round is high-leverage (e.g., preventing opponent match point).
- **Bonus**: keeping cheap-but-real guns (Spectre/Judge) after a pistol win instead
  of buying rifles, so a loss doesn't hurt the bank.
- **Anti-eco**: playing against broke opponents — buy utility, hold range against
  shotguns/pistols, don't peek dry into stacked close angles.

## Team economy rules of thumb

- Call buys as a team in the buy phase: "full", "force", or "save" — five matching
  buys win more rounds than five individually-optimal buys.
- Drop weapons for broke teammates when you're above ~6,000 credits; a 5-rifle
  round beats one player sitting on max bank.
- Track the enemy economy: after you win a gun round, expect an eco — after their
  eco, expect a full buy with loss bonus. Their post-plant saves tell you what
  they'll have next round.
- Losing a round with an Operator (4,700) swings economy harder than any other
  death. Op players must play for the exit if the round is lost.
- On match point against you, there is no next round to save for — force buy.

## Common economy mistakes

- Force buying every round out of frustration (tilted spending) — this creates
  permanent rifle disadvantage.
- Buying a rifle while skipping shields against a full-buy enemy: heavy shield
  changes many duels from 1-tap-loss to survivable.
- Saving a Spectre by hiding while 4 teammates fight a 4v5 — the loss bonus you
  denied them usually outweighs the 1,600 you saved.
- Ignoring the plant: a lost round with a plant (+300 x5) plus loss bonus often
  funds a full buy that a plantless round wouldn't.
