# Role Guide — What Each Role Actually Does

Role responsibilities are stable across patches even as agent tiers move.

## Duelist — the space taker

- Job: create space by taking the first fight of an execute. Your team converts
  the space; your death is acceptable ONLY if it's traded and produces map
  territory.
- Attack: you swing first onto the site once your initiators have thrown their
  utility. Waiting for someone else to go first as a duelist is a role failure.
- Defense: you're the aggressive info-taker — early picks with an escape plan
  (Jett dash, Raze satchel), then reposition.
- Common mistakes: entrying without utility or a trade partner behind you;
  lurking on a duelist (that's a Yoru/Reyna exception, not the norm); saving
  your movement ability "for later" and dying with it.
- KPI: first-kill attempts and entry success, not raw KDA. A 15/15 duelist who
  opened 10 sites did their job.

## Initiator — the fight starter

- Job: break defensive holds with recon and flashes so your duelist's swing is
  a favorable fight. You are the second player through the door, trading the
  duelist.
- Attack: sequence matters — recon (find the anchor) → flash/stun (break the
  angle) → duelist swings → you trade. Utility dumped 10 seconds early just
  tells defenders you're coming.
- Defense: use recon to confirm site hits early for rotations; flash to delay
  a rush while your team arrives.
- Common mistakes: using the drone/recon and then not following your duelist;
  holding all utility to the point the round ends with 4 unused abilities.
- KPI: assists, first-kill participation, and how often your executes get the
  spike down.

## Controller — the map divider

- Job: cut enemy sightlines so your team crosses the map without being shot
  from three angles. Smokes are the tempo control of the whole game.
- Attack: smoke the standard defensive angles on the execute (e.g., CT and
  the close cubby), and re-smoke for the post-plant. Save one smoke for after
  the plant whenever possible.
- Defense: smoke to slow the hit (choke smokes buy 15+ seconds), one-way
  setups where you know them, and delay retakes with your area denial.
- Common mistakes: smoking on cooldown with no plan; smoking your own team's
  refresh angles on retake; playing controller like a duelist and dying with
  smokes unused.
- KPI: rounds where your team took or held space without deaths, post-plant
  conversion rate.

## Sentinel — the anchor

- Job: hold a site (defense) or the team's back (attack) with trips, turrets
  and objects, converting your utility into time and information.
- Defense: your setup should let you survive contact — the goal is to delay a
  hit for 10–20 seconds while the rotation arrives, not to win a 1v5.
- Attack: watch the flank with trips/cams so four teammates can play forward;
  join late as the extra gun or hold the lurk.
- Common mistakes: same trap spots every round (good enemies clear them by
  round 4); dying before your utility even triggers; anchoring so passively
  you never re-take space when the site is clearly safe.
- KPI: rounds where the site hit was delayed or the flank was called, deaths
  after (not before) your utility did its work.

## Flex and IGL notes

- Every team needs a fifth player who fills the comp gap — usually second
  controller or second initiator by map. Flexing well is the most underrated
  ranked skill because you always play the missing role, so the comp always
  works.
- Someone must make round calls: a mediocre plan executed by five beats a
  perfect plan executed by two. In solo queue, being the calm voice that says
  "default, B hit at 45 seconds" wins games by itself.

## Building a 5-stack comp

- Baseline template: 1 duelist, 1 initiator, 1 controller, 1 sentinel, +1 flex
  chosen by map (second initiator on open maps, second controller on maps like
  Icebox/Breeze, second duelist on tight fast maps).
- Prefer comfort over theory-crafting: a player's best agent on the "wrong"
  role usually outperforms their third-best on the "right" one — but never
  queue without at least one smoke player.
