# Valorant Maps — Pool, Identities and Core Callouts

Snapshot from training data (late 2025 / early 2026). The competitive pool is
7 maps and rotates roughly every Act, so check the client for the live pool.

## Map pool basics

- The competitive/pro pool holds 7 maps at a time; maps rotate in and out each Act.
- Around late 2025 the rotation included newer maps Abyss (June 2024) and Corrode
  (June 2025) alongside classics like Ascent, Bind and Haven.
- Non-pool maps remain playable in Swiftplay/Unrated/customs.
- Learning 2–3 pool maps deeply beats knowing all maps shallowly for ranked climbing.

## Ascent

- Identity: mid-control map. Whoever owns mid (Catwalk, Market, Pizza) can hit
  either site or cut rotations.
- Unique mechanic: closable metal doors on A and B site that can be destroyed.
- Key callouts: A Main, A Tree (Wine), Heaven, Hell; Mid Courtyard, Catwalk, Market;
  B Main, Lane (Boathouse side), CT, Logs.
- Classic comp anchors: Killjoy B, Omen smokes, Sova/Fade recon, Jett Op mid.

## Bind

- Identity: no mid. Two sites linked by one-way teleporters (Hookah TP to B Long
  exit near A, and A/B rotations through spawn).
- Fakes are extremely strong because teleporters make rotations loud and readable.
- Key callouts: A Short (Cubby), A Lamps (Lobby), A Tower/Heaven, U-Hall; B Long
  (Garden), B Hookah (Window), Elbow, B Hall.
- Brimstone/Raze thrive; post-plant molly lineups are a big part of Bind.

## Haven

- Identity: the original three-site map (A, B, C). Defenders are stretched thin,
  so info and fast rotations decide rounds.
- Key callouts: A Long, A Short (Sewer), A Tower; B site with Mid Window and Mid
  Doors; C Long, C Garage (with window), C Cubby.
- Attackers can split through Garage/Mid to hit B or C; defenders often stack info
  utility on flanks.

## Split

- Identity: tight, vertical, defender-sided historically. Ropes/ascenders on both
  sites and mid. Mid control unlocks everything.
- Key callouts: A Main, A Ramp, A Heaven, A Screens; Mid Top, Mid Mail, Vent, Sewer;
  B Main, B Heaven, B Rafters, Garage.
- Raze, Breach, Cypher and Sage (mid wall) are traditional Split staples.

## Icebox

- Identity: vertical fights, ziplines, long sightlines into close angles. Plant
  spots matter enormously (default behind the B green wall, A on top of Rafters
  boxes).
- Key callouts: A Belt, A Nest, A Rafters, A Screen; Mid Blue, Boiler, Tube; B Long
  (Yellow), B Green, Kitchen, Snowman.
- Viper wall is near-mandatory for site executes; Sage wall enables A plants.

## Breeze

- Identity: the biggest, most open map. Long ranges favor Operators, Guardians and
  precise rifling. Fewer close-quarter fights than any other map.
- Key callouts: A Cave, A Shop, A Pyramids; Mid Net, Mid Wood Doors, Mid Nest,
  Chute (tube drop); B Main, B Elbow, B Tunnel (double doors).
- Viper, Sova, KAY/O and a chamber-style Op agent historically define Breeze comps.

## Fracture

- Identity: attackers can hit every site from two directions at once (spawn ziplines
  wrap the map). Four ult orbs. Defenders face crossfire setups constantly.
- Key callouts: A Site with A Rope, A Dish, A Drop; B Tower, B Arcade, B Canteen,
  B Tree; Dish and Tower are the power positions.
- Breach and Brimstone are classically strong; timing double-side hits is the
  whole attacking game plan.

## Pearl

- Identity: underwater city, two sites, no gimmick mechanics — a pure geometry map
  with a huge mid (Plaza, Connector) and a long B main.
- Key callouts: A Main, A Art, A Dugout, A Flowers/Secret; Mid Shops, Mid Plaza,
  Connector; B Main (Ramp), B Hall, B Screen, B Tower.
- Slower, structured play; controllers with long smokes and post-plant utility do well.

## Lotus

- Identity: three sites like Haven, plus rotating stone doors (A–B and B–C), a
  destructible wall between A and A Top, and a silent one-way drop from A to
  defender side.
- Key callouts: A Main, A Root, A Tree, A Top; B Main, B Upper, B Pillar; C Main,
  C Mound, C Bend, Waterfall.
- Rotating doors are loud and one-way tools for fakes; initiator-heavy comps thrive.

## Sunset

- Identity: Los Angeles-themed, compact two-site map with a straightforward
  three-lane layout. The B site has a closable metal door in Mid (Mid Courtyard).
- Key callouts: A Main, A Elbow, A Top, A Alley; Mid Tiles, Mid Bottom, Mid Courtyard;
  B Main, B Boba, B Sites' Market connector.
- Fights are frequent and close; flashes and fast trades matter more than Op angles.

## Abyss

- Identity: released June 2024. The first map with no perimeter walls — players can
  fall off the map edges to their death. Verticality and risky edge boosts create
  unique angles.
- Key callouts: A Main, A Tower, A Secret; Mid Top, Mid Bottom, Mid Library; B Main,
  B Tower, B Danger (edge walkways).
- Movement agents can cross gaps; falling deaths are a real economy risk.

## Corrode

- Identity: released June 2025. Two-site map introduced during Season 2025; compact
  layout with contested mid control. As a newer map, callout conventions and meta
  comps were still settling at the time of this snapshot — details here may be
  incomplete or outdated.
- Early sentiment favored initiator-heavy clears and standard Omen/Cypher cores
  while teams learned the map.

## Picking what to learn

- For ranked: master the current pool's most-picked maps first (historically Ascent,
  Haven and Bind appear most in queue when in rotation).
- Learn one default attack setup and one default defense setup per map before
  learning lineups.
- Callout fluency is a free rank-up: 5 seconds of clear comms beats perfect aim
  you can't coordinate.
