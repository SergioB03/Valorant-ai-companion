# Improvement Fundamentals — Aim, Crosshair Placement and Review

Timeless improvement mechanics for Valorant. None of this changes with patches.

## Crosshair placement (the highest-ROI skill)

- Keep the crosshair at head height at all times — including while walking
  through cleared space. Head height on most maps is roughly the top of the
  shoulder-level boxes and rails; learn it per map, per position.
- Pre-aim the exact pixel where a head will appear before you swing an angle,
  so the fight is a click, not a flick.
- Hug distance matters: keep the crosshair off the wall you're passing —
  aim where the enemy will be, typically a few degrees off the corner.
- Most low-elo deaths are crosshair-at-chest or crosshair-at-floor deaths.
  Fixing placement alone is commonly worth a full rank.

## Counter-strafing and first-bullet discipline

- Valorant guns are accurate only when you are (nearly) still. Tap the opposite
  movement key (or simply release keys — Valorant decelerates fast) before
  firing.
- First-bullet accuracy on rifles is very high standing still and terrible
  while moving; win fights by shooting second-but-accurate rather than
  first-but-sprayed.
- Learn one spray pattern well (Vandal or Phantom) for close range, but default
  to bursts of 1–3 at distance.
- Vandal vs Phantom: Vandal one-taps heads at any range; Phantom has no tracers,
  a faster fire rate, and wins sprays up close but falls off at long range.
  Pick per map/role rather than by loyalty.

## A daily aim routine (20 minutes)

- 5 min Range: 30-bot flick drill at medium difficulty, then strafing-bots for
  counter-strafe timing.
- 10 min Deathmatch with rules: rifle only, always counter-strafe, always
  re-position after 2 kills, crosshair head-height between fights. Ignore the
  scoreboard.
- 5 min of your weakness: Sheriff-only DM (first-bullet), or Guardian-only
  (taps), or an aim-trainer scenario for micro-adjustments.
- Consistency beats volume: 20 focused minutes daily outperforms a 3-hour
  Saturday session.

## Game sense: the minimap habit

- Glance at the minimap every 5–10 seconds and after every teammate death.
  Most "he came out of nowhere" deaths were on the minimap for free.
- Count enemies: every kill callout updates your model of where the remaining
  players can physically be, given timing.
- Learn common defaults: where each rank of player watches from on each site,
  and the standard rotation timings between sites.

## Utility efficiency

- Utility exists to convert aim duels into unfair fights. Grade each ability
  use: did it produce info, space, or a kill? Random smokes and panic flashes
  are negative-value.
- Entry rule: flash/recon BEFORE the duelist swings, not after he's dead.
- Save one defensive ability for post-plant/retake when possible — a retake
  with zero utility against a set defense is close to a coin flip at best.

## VOD review that actually improves you

- Review losses, not wins, and review within a day while you remember intent.
- One pass, first-person, at 1.5x speed. Pause on every death and ask exactly
  two questions: "What information did I ignore?" and "What would the better
  decision have been 5 seconds earlier?"
- Log mistakes in categories (dry peek, no trade setup, bad timing, eco error,
  crosshair). After 5 games, your top category is your training focus — not
  whatever tilted you most.
- Watch one pro/high-elo VOD per week on your main agent and map, focusing on
  their positioning between fights rather than their kills.

## Fastest structural wins

- Play a small pool: one role, two to three agents, and the current map pool.
  Spreading across roles halves your rate of pattern learning.
- Fix your setup once: a comfortable sensitivity (many find 0.3–0.45 at 800
  DPI territory a sane starting zone), crosshair you never change again,
  capped stable FPS.
- Talk: even solo queue, simple comms (enemy positions, your plan) measurably
  raise round win rates. Silent lobbies lose to average ones.
- Warm up before ranked, never during game one — see the mental-game notes on
  warmup structure.
